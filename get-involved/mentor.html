Why mentorship matters
WHY MENTOR?

Talent grows faster when experience is shared.

A mentor does not have to have all the answers. What matters is the willingness to listen, share honestly, ask useful questions, offer perspective and help another person navigate the decisions that come with developing a skill.

For CoWiTa, mentorship connects a young person's creative journey with people who can contribute knowledge, experience, discipline, encouragement and perspective along the way.

OUR APPROACH

We believe mentorship should help a young person move forward.

Mentorship should not become a one-way lecture. The strongest relationships create space for questions, practice, reflection, honest feedback and gradual independence.

A CoWiTa mentor may contribute to a creative discipline, a practical skill, personal development, career thinking, creative entrepreneurship or simply the confidence to keep developing.

01

Listen first

Understand the young person's interests, goals, challenges and current level before trying to direct the journey.

02

Guide, don't control

Offer perspective and challenge while allowing the mentee to develop ownership of their own decisions.

03

Build independence

The goal is not dependence on a mentor, but greater confidence, ability and direction over time.

WHO CAN MENTOR?

There is more than one way to bring value.

You do not need to fit one specific professional profile. What matters is having something useful to share and the willingness to contribute responsibly.

Creative Practitioners

Artists and creatives who have developed experience in their craft and can help young people understand the discipline behind creative growth.

  • Dance and performance
  • Music and songwriting
  • Drawing and visual art
  • Writing and storytelling
  • Acting and theatre

Industry Professionals

People working in creative, technical or professional fields who can expose young people to how their skills operate beyond the classroom.

  • Creative production
  • Media and communication
  • Technology and digital work
  • Business and entrepreneurship
  • Professional development

Experienced Community Members

People with practical experience, leadership ability or community knowledge who can provide perspective and encouragement.

  • Leadership and responsibility
  • Personal development
  • Life skills
  • Community engagement
  • Purpose and direction

People Who Have Walked the Journey

Sometimes the most valuable mentor is someone who remembers what it felt like to be at the beginning and can share the lessons they wish they had known earlier.

  • Emerging creatives
  • Young professionals
  • Entrepreneurs
  • Former participants
  • Peer mentors

From experience to meaningful guidance.

02

WHAT TO EXPECT

Mentorship is a commitment.

The exact format will depend on the programme and the needs of the mentee. The principles below provide the foundation for how CoWiTa intends to approach the relationship.

Consistent engagement

Meaningful mentorship requires more than a single inspirational conversation. Consistency helps trust and progress develop over time.

Honest feedback

Mentors should be able to encourage without simply agreeing. Useful feedback helps a young person recognise strengths and areas that still need work.

Practical guidance

Where appropriate, mentorship should connect ideas to action: practice, projects, portfolios, performances, learning or other meaningful next steps.

Respect for the mentee

Every young person arrives with a different background, personality, pace and ambition. Mentorship should create room for that individuality.

Professional boundaries

Mentorship should remain purposeful, respectful and appropriately bounded, particularly when working with children and younger participants.

A willingness to learn

Mentors can learn too. Strong mentorship is a relationship where experience is shared while both people remain open to new perspectives.

WHAT WE VALUE IN A MENTOR

You do not need to be perfect. You need to be intentional.

A mentor's value is not measured only by titles, qualifications or years of experience. Character, reliability and the ability to genuinely listen can be just as important.

Safeguarding matters.

CoWiTa's work may involve children and young people. Any future mentorship programme will need clear safeguarding expectations, appropriate boundaries, responsible communication and suitable processes for working with minors. Participation in mentorship will therefore be subject to the requirements of the specific programme.

QUESTIONS

Before you express interest.

Do I need to be a professional artist to become a mentor?

No. Creative professionals are one possible source of mentorship, but useful experience can also come from entrepreneurship, leadership, technical work, education, community work and other areas. What matters is whether your experience can meaningfully support a young person's development.

Can I mentor in a specific creative discipline?

Yes. Depending on future programme needs, mentors may contribute to areas such as dance, music, drawing, writing, acting, creative production, digital skills and other relevant areas.

Do I need previous mentoring experience?

Not necessarily. Previous mentoring experience can be valuable, but CoWiTa's approach will also consider your experience, communication ability, willingness to learn and suitability for the role.

Will mentors work with children?

Some CoWiTa programmes may involve children or younger participants. Where this applies, additional safeguarding, communication and participation requirements will apply.

Can I contribute even if I have limited time?

Potentially. Different mentorship opportunities may require different levels of commitment. The exact formats and time requirements will depend on the programme being developed.

Is the mentor programme currently open?

Formal mentor intake information is currently coming soon. You can still express your interest through the Contact page so CoWiTa can understand the experience and skills available as the pathway develops.
