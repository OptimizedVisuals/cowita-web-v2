Why mentorship matters

Talent development is not only about learning a skill.

Young people also need people who can help them understand what their skills can become.

A young dancer may know how to perform but not know how to build a professional practice. A musician may have the ability but lack someone who can provide honest feedback. A young writer may have ideas but need guidance on how to develop them into work that can reach an audience.

Mentorship creates space for that relationship to develop. It allows experience to be shared, questions to be asked, mistakes to become learning opportunities and ambition to become more practical.

At CoWiTa, mentorship is therefore part of the pathway rather than an activity that exists separately from talent development.

Young people should not have to figure everything out alone.

Access to someone with relevant experience can change how a young person sees their own potential.

The role of a mentor is not to decide a young person's future for them. It is to help them think more clearly, develop their ability, understand possibilities and make better decisions.

CoWiTa seeks to build mentorship relationships around trust, learning, accountability and practical growth.

What mentorship provides

More than advice.

Effective mentorship can give a young person access to perspective, constructive feedback and a clearer view of what comes next.

01

Perspective

Help young people understand their strengths, challenges and possibilities from a wider perspective.

02

Feedback

Provide constructive guidance that helps creative work and practical skills improve over time.

03

Confidence

Create a supportive relationship where young people can ask questions, experiment and learn from mistakes.

04

Direction

Help participants think about realistic next steps for developing their creative journey.

05

Exposure

Where appropriate, mentors can introduce participants to new perspectives, environments and professional possibilities.

06

Accountability

Encourage participants to keep learning, practising and following through on the goals they set.

The mentorship pathway

A relationship built around growth.

CoWiTa's mentorship model is being developed to connect the right people around the needs and goals of each participant.

01

Understand

Begin by understanding the participant's talent, interests, goals and current level of development.

02

Match

Where appropriate, connect the participant with a mentor whose experience and strengths are relevant to their development.

03

Develop

Create a relationship around learning, practice, feedback and practical development.

04

Connect

Where opportunities are available, mentorship can help participants understand and access wider creative and professional networks.

05

Continue

Growth does not end with one conversation. CoWiTa's longer-term goal is to build relationships that encourage continued development.

Who mentorship serves

Different stages. Different needs.

Mentorship can take different forms depending on where a young person is in their journey.

Emerging talent Young people discovering what they can do and looking for direction.
Developing creatives Participants who already have a skill and want to strengthen it.
Young creators Creatives beginning to produce work and build confidence in their practice.
Aspiring professionals Young people exploring how their creative skills can connect to real opportunities.
